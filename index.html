What it does

Works through a batch. Load a whole set at once, including HEIC straight from an iPhone. Approve one and it saves itself into the folder you chose, then opens the next.

Three ways to cover. Blur, mosaic or a solid block, painted freehand or dropped as a rectangle or a circle you can move, resize and rotate. A bar across a pair of eyes and nothing else takes a second.

Removes what the picture does not show. Every saved file is re-encoded from scratch, which drops GPS coordinates, timestamps, camera and lens serial numbers, orientation flags and the embedded preview thumbnail, which can otherwise still hold an unedited copy of the original.

Leaves your originals alone. The output is a new file next to the old one. Nothing on your disk is modified.

Worth knowing

Blur and mosaic can be partly reversed. Both keep a statistical trace of what they covered, and on text, registration plates and small faces that trace is sometimes enough to reconstruct the original. Where it genuinely matters, use the solid block, and judge the result at full zoom rather than from the fitted view.

Painting is destructive. A mark replaces the pixels underneath rather than sitting over them, so there is nothing to peel off later, in the saved file or on screen.

Writing into a folder needs Chrome or Edge. Any other browser falls back to your downloads folder. Everything else works the same either way.
